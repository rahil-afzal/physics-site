## Status of each unit

- **Core:** scheduled and taught in full (units U1–U9).
- **Provisional:** scheduled, but the departmental requirement is unconfirmed (U10, Waves and Interference).
- **Unresolved:** not scheduled; the departmental requirement is unconfirmed (U11, Elasticity, Surface Tension, Terminal Velocity). Its absence from the schedule is not a decision that it is outside the course.

## Revision 2 decisions

- Newton's laws and force concepts come before the derivation of centripetal force.
- SHM is taught through a physical derivation and the sinusoidal solution first, before any more advanced differential-equation methods.
- LC and RLC circuits form their own unit, explicitly linked to the mechanical oscillations.
- Waves and interference are provisionally included, pending department confirmation.

## Course outcomes

As printed on the final examination papers (paraphrased):

- **CO1.** Define different physical quantities with examples.
- **CO2.** Derive or show the various equations of SHM, damped harmonic motion, wave motion, and so on.
- **CO3.** Evaluate numerical problems based on the basic characteristics of SHM and damped harmonic motion.

## Course description (provisional)

A first-year, calculus-based introductory physics course for engineering students. It develops the mechanics needed for engineering — vectors, kinematics and projectile motion, Newton's laws, circular motion, work, energy and torque, and fluid mechanics — and then builds a connected treatment of oscillations: simple harmonic motion developed from uniform circular motion, damped oscillations, and LC and RLC circuits, followed by waves and interference. The course emphasises physical meaning, the derivation of key results, and numerical problem solving.

## Learning objectives (provisional)

By the end of the course a student should be able to:

1. Resolve, add and multiply vectors, and use them to describe forces, displacements, work and torque.
2. Describe motion in one and two dimensions, and analyse projectile and uniform circular motion.
3. Apply Newton's laws with free-body diagrams, and relate centripetal force to circular motion.
4. Apply the work–energy theorem and conservation of mechanical energy, and compute torque.
5. Apply the continuity equation and Bernoulli's equation to ideal fluid flow, and classify flow using the Reynolds number.
6. Derive and use the equations of SHM, including its energy, for the spring–mass system and the simple pendulum.
7. Derive the equation of damped oscillation, classify its regimes, and compute quantities for underdamped motion.
8. Derive and apply the LC and RLC circuit equations using the mechanical–electrical analogy.
9. Derive the travelling-wave equation, distinguish particle and wave velocity, and analyse interference and Young's double-slit fringes.
10. Solve quantitative problems of the kinds set in past examinations, with correct units and physical interpretation.

## Mathematical background (assumed, not verified)

Trigonometry; basic differentiation and integration; quadratic equations and the discriminant; exponentials and natural logarithms; the small-angle approximation. Not assumed: partial derivatives (treated informally in Waves), formal differential-equation methods (only trial solutions are used), and any circuit knowledge (a primer is part of Class 19).

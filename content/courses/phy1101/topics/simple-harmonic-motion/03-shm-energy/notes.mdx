## Learning Goals

- Derive $E=\tfrac12kA^2$.
- Justify that the total energy is constant while kinetic and potential energy vary.
- Sketch the energy graphs.

## Kinetic, potential and total energy

$$U=\tfrac12kx^2,\qquad K=\tfrac12mv^2=\tfrac12m\omega^2(A^2-x^2),\qquad E=\tfrac12kA^2=\tfrac12m\omega^2A^2$$

### Derivation: total energy

Using $x=A\sin\omega t$, $v=\omega A\cos\omega t$ and $k=m\omega^2$:

$$E=K+U=\tfrac12kA^2\left(\cos^2\omega t+\sin^2\omega t\right)=\tfrac12kA^2$$

## Energy as a function of time

$$K=\tfrac12kA^2\cos^2\omega t,\qquad U=\tfrac12kA^2\sin^2\omega t$$

## Energy as a function of displacement

The potential energy is a parabola in $x$, and the total energy is proportional to the square of the amplitude.

## Learning Goals

- Derive the SHM equation for the spring–mass system.
- Use initial conditions to find the amplitude and phase.
- Derive the simple-pendulum equation and state when the SHM model is valid.

## Spring-mass system

From this page the spring axis is called $x$, to match the examination papers; the solution has the same form as $y=A\sin\omega t$.

$$F=-kx,\qquad m\ddot x=-kx\ \Rightarrow\ \ddot x+\omega_0^2x=0,\qquad \omega_0=\sqrt{\frac km},\qquad T=2\pi\sqrt{\frac mk}$$

$$x=A\sin(\omega_0t+\varphi)$$

### Derivation: the SHM equation

From Newton's second law and Hooke's law. The sinusoidal solution is verified by substitution first; no advanced differential-equation methods are used.

## Initial conditions

$$A=\sqrt{x_0^2+\frac{v_0^2}{\omega_0^2}},\qquad \tan\varphi=\frac{\omega_0x_0}{v_0},\qquad \omega_0^2=-\frac ax$$

Release from rest at $x=A$ requires $\varphi=\pi/2$, so $x=A\cos\omega_0t$.

<Callout type="note" title="Phase">
Starting at $y=0$ gives $y=A\sin\omega t$, while many problems release the block from rest at the amplitude, which requires $\varphi=\pi/2$.
</Callout>

## Simple pendulum

$$mL\ddot\theta=-mg\sin\theta\ \xrightarrow{\ \sin\theta\approx\theta\ }\ \ddot\theta+\frac gL\theta=0,\qquad \omega=\sqrt{\frac gL},\qquad T=2\pi\sqrt{\frac Lg}$$

### Derivation: the pendulum equation

The tangential component of gravity is the restoring force; the small-angle approximation reduces the equation to the SHM form.

The pendulum is only approximately SHM, valid for small angles. In that limit the period is independent of mass and amplitude.

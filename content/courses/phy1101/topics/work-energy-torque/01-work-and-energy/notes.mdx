## Learning Goals

- Compute work done by constant and variable forces.
- Apply the work–energy theorem.
- Apply conservation of mechanical energy.

## Work by a force

$$W=\vec F\cdot\vec d=Fd\cos\theta,\qquad W=\int\vec F\cdot d\vec r$$

*(to be written)*

## Kinetic energy and the work-energy theorem

$$K=\tfrac12mv^2,\qquad W_{\text{net}}=\Delta K$$

### Derivation: one dimension

$$W=\int F\,dx=\int mv\,dv=\tfrac12mv_f^2-\tfrac12mv_i^2$$

*(to be written)*

## Work done by a spring

$$W_{\text{spring}}=-\tfrac12kx^2$$

The work done by the spring on the mass, as it is stretched or compressed from its natural length by $x$.

*(to be written)*

## Potential energy and conservation of energy

$$\Delta U=-W_{\text{cons}},\qquad U_g=mgh,\qquad U_s=\tfrac12kx^2,\qquad K+U=\text{const}$$

Potential energy is defined for conservative forces. Mechanical energy is conserved when only conservative forces do work. Energy diagrams are a preview of SHM.

## Learning Goals

- Derive the damped equation of motion and the regime conditions.
- Classify an oscillator from $m$, $k$ and $b$.
- Compute the critical value of $b$.

<Callout type="warning" title="Provisional conventions">
The damping constant is written $\gamma=b/2m$ here. The department's symbol, the definition of lifetime, and the sine or cosine form of the underdamped solution are still to be matched.
</Callout>

## Equation of motion

$$m\ddot x+b\dot x+kx=0\ \Rightarrow\ \ddot x+2\gamma\dot x+\omega_0^2x=0,\qquad \gamma=\frac{b}{2m},\quad \omega_0^2=\frac km$$

### Derivation: the damped equation

From Newton's second law with the resistive force $F_d=-bv$.

*(to be written)*

## Characteristic equation

$$x=e^{\lambda t}:\quad \lambda^2+2\gamma\lambda+\omega_0^2=0,\qquad \lambda=-\gamma\pm\sqrt{\gamma^2-\omega_0^2}$$

*(to be written)*

## The three regimes

| Regime | Condition | Solution form |
|---|---|---|
| Underdamped (oscillatory) | $\gamma<\omega_0$, i.e. $b<2\sqrt{mk}$ | $x=A_0e^{-\gamma t}\sin(\omega' t+\varphi)$ |
| Critically damped | $\gamma=\omega_0$, i.e. $b=2\sqrt{mk}$ | $x=(C_1+C_2t)e^{-\gamma t}$ |
| Overdamped | $\gamma>\omega_0$, i.e. $b>2\sqrt{mk}$ | $x=C_1e^{\lambda_+t}+C_2e^{\lambda_-t}$, both $\lambda$ real and negative |

The sign of $\gamma^2-\omega_0^2$ decides whether the roots are complex, repeated or real. Critical damping is the fastest return to equilibrium without oscillation. The solutions are stated here; the underdamped case is examined on the next page.

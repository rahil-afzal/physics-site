## The travelling-wave equation

$$y(x,t)=A\sin(\omega t-kx)\ \ (+x\ \text{direction}),\qquad y=A\sin(\omega t+kx)\ \ (-x\ \text{direction})$$

### Derivation: a delayed source

The source is $y(0,t)=A\sin\omega t$ (SHM page 1). A point at $x$ lags by $x/v$, so
$y(x,t)=A\sin\omega(t-x/v)=A\sin(\omega t-kx)$ with $k=\omega/v$.

<Callout type="warning" title="Provisional convention">
The sign convention $y=A\sin(\omega t-kx)$ follows from the source $y=A\sin\omega t$. It is still to be matched to the department's convention.
</Callout>

## Particle velocity and wave velocity

$$v_p=\frac{\partial y}{\partial t}=\omega A\cos(\omega t-kx),\qquad v_{p,\max}=\omega A$$

The particle velocity differs from the wave speed $v=\omega/k$. The partial derivative is treated informally: differentiate in $t$ holding $x$ fixed. The initial phase comes from the initial conditions; for example, a particle at maximum displacement with zero velocity gives $\varphi=\pi/2$.
